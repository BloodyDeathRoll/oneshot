# industry.md — purpose-driven brand development in Israel, for מותג מיטיב (Mutag Meitiv)

Merged 2026-10-18 from the five lane files and `skeptic.md` (78 claims: 42 survive, 35 weakened, 1 killed). Only SURVIVES and WEAKENED claims are cited; WEAKENED claims are stated in the skeptic's narrowed form. No fact here is new.

## The industry and how it makes money

- In a global, self-selected 2023 survey of 267 branding professionals (mostly self-employed or founders), 69 % charge fixed project prices and 19.9 % use retainers; consulting (18.8 %) and digital products (16.4 %) are the commonest secondary revenue streams, and 16.2 % have no other stream. (C-industry-01)
- Visible Israeli price shapes are few and come from single providers:
  - one branding consultant sells 1:1 guidance as a prepaid 10-hour bank at ₪7,500 + VAT (about ₪750/h), with the final hours set by scope (C-industry-02);
  - one Hebrew provider, Brand Academy, lists a 10-session recorded branding course at ₪1,050 + VAT with a ₪420 sale, digital courses at ₪1,111–1,239 and a brand-manager course at ₪13,500, on an undated page that may date from 2021 (C-industry-03);
  - two Israeli branding lecturers sell talks (~50 min) and workshops (~90 min) to organisations and employees with no public price ("contact us") (C-industry-05);
  - among the studios opened, the visible shekel prices are on courses (Rinat Hadar ₪4,400 incl. VAT, ₪3,900 early, 10 × 2.5 h Zoom, max 10; Mateg ₪1,690, down from ₪2,490) and single agency line items; no studio shows a project price (C-players-04).
- A free, 3-hour, self-paced state course on personal branding exists (Campus IL, Ministry of Labor with partners, live 2026-10). (C-industry-04)
- Free and subsidised small-business advice is searched for in Hebrew ("בחינם" free, "מחיר" price, "מעוף" MAOF completions, 2026-10); one accelerator (The Hive) offered growth-strategy support in 2020. The effect on paid demand is unknown. (C-audience-10)
- The climate is hard for the buyers:
  - about 61,000 Israeli businesses closed in 2025 and about 37,500 opened, mostly SMBs (CofaceBDI via Mako) (C-industry-06);
  - 54 % of the self-employed reported lower activity since the war, down about 50 % on average (IDI, survey 2024-12, n = 1,236 with 194 self-employed) (C-industry-07);
  - 71 % of Israeli nonprofits report financial difficulty, 40 % say foreign support fell and 18 % cannot meet monthly payments (Hebrew University survey 2025, n = 170, via Calcalist) (C-industry-08).
  - None of this shows anyone buying branding; it is strain, not demand (skeptic, Pain vs WTP).
- Purpose framing is shifting: per The Drum (2025-06) reporting Edelman's 2025 brand-trust report, trust now equals price and quality in purchase decisions, and "me" expectations (feel good, hope, information: 68 % each) outrank "help me do good" (61 %) and "help the community" (59 %); sample unknown, Israel not shown to be included. (C-industry-09)
- Kantar (reported 2025-11) says 60 % of consumers believe companies use green messaging purely for commercial ends; global, sample not stated. (C-industry-10)
- From 27 September 2026 the EU bans unproven generic green claims, offset-based neutrality claims and uncertified sustainability labels, for traders wherever they are established; whether the client's buyers sell B2C into the EU is unproven. (C-industry-11)
- As of 2023, ESG and climate reporting by Israeli listed companies was voluntary (ISA recommended outline 2021; no TASE mandate); since 2022 the ISA has required fund managers to integrate ESG; status for 2024–26 not established. (C-industry-12)
- As of 2021, four Israeli companies were certified B Corps and B Lab had no Israeli partner; there is no current count, and nothing supports "B Corp growth in Israel". (C-industry-13)
- In Figma's 2025 survey of 2,500 users (product designers, not brand strategists), 78 % say AI makes them more efficient, but only 31 % of designers use it for core work and 32 % can depend on its output. (C-industry-14)
- LinkedIn reaches about 2.95 M Israelis (30.7 %, ad-audience estimate, 2026-09), with 25–34 the largest group (47.5 %). (C-industry-15, C-audience-07)

## Sites in this space and what converts

No conversion data was found for any site; everything below is the structure of admired or comparable sites, not proof of what converts (skeptic, Pain vs WTP).

**Large studios (11 sites: 7 international, 4 Israeli)**
- The first screen is one short position sentence; in 10 of 11 the services list starts on the second screen or later (Imaginet leads with its niche). (C-sites-01)
- Case studies are large image tiles with client, a one-line problem or outcome and service tags, the full story one tap away; none puts long case text on the home page. (C-sites-02)
- A broad offer is split into named groups (divisions such as Futerra's four, or client situations such as Collins's 11 "Programs"), never a flat list; top-level navs have 4–7 items. (C-sites-03)
- All four Israeli studio sites in the sample put a contact action on the first mobile screen; none of the seven international studios did. (C-sites-04)
- International studios ask for an email to an office inbox; the three Hebrew studios offer phone and/or WhatsApp plus a long form (8–11 fields at Imaginet and NotFromHere). (C-sites-05)
- Newsletter capture is footer-only and email-only; none of the 11 showed a newsletter pop-up at 390 px. (C-sites-06)
- A home-page process section is the exception (3 of 11), and when present it is 3–4 short named steps or values. (C-sites-07)
- Trust is shown through client logos, awards and named testimonials; NotFromHere, Imaginet and iDeez use logo walls, and two of them also use testimonial sets. (C-sites-08)
- Weight: in one unthrottled headless run (390 px, load + 4 s), the three video-reel home pages (Wolff Olins, Dumbar, Koto) moved 20–42 MB against 1.2–3.7 MB for the four lightest; only NotFromHere came in under 1.5 MB. Indicative order, not a measured first load. (C-sites-09)
- Of three Hebrew studio sites, two set mobile body text at about 14 px and one at 17 px, all with line-height 1.5–1.7; the three international sites sampled run tighter (1.05–1.2). (C-sites-10)
- Hebrew studio sites keep Latin to brand names and jargon and mirror directional arrows (forward points left); there is no logical property for `translateX`, so hover nudges need an explicit RTL flip. (C-sites-11)
- Four motion patterns seen in still captures — floating pill nav, sticky wordmark with a scroll-lit list, orbiting labels, blur-to-focus reveal — are low-byte and compatible with NN/g's 100–500 ms guidance; the fifth, a scroll-scrubbed video reel with smooth-scroll, is not, given NN/g's scrolljacking findings. Pattern 4 may be a lazy-load placeholder. (C-sites-12)
- Cookie banners cover about 35–40 % of the first mobile screen on Wolff Olins, Futerra and Imaginet (headless, this machine). (C-sites-13)
- Israeli accessibility regulations (Reg. 35) require covered sites to meet IS 5568, legally anchored to WCAG 2.0 AA (current edition 2023, exact WCAG alignment unverified), and to publish a prominent accessibility statement; an overlay plugin alone does not comply; turnover exemptions exist but their thresholds are unverified. (C-sites-14)
- Top international studios build on a headless CMS with a JS framework (Next.js/Nuxt + Sanity); 3 of 4 Israeli studios are on WordPress or Webflow. (C-sites-15)

**Small Hebrew practices (6 sites the client's buyers compare with: DoGood, Tali Gilad, Shelly Sever, Reuta, Galit Sharon, Rinat Hadar)**
- The two coaches (Reuta, Galit Sharon) lead with the founder's name and role line, the two design studios (DoGood, Rinat Hadar) lead with a position line, and two (Tali Gilad at 390 px, Shelly Sever) show no offer sentence on the first mobile screen; four of six show the founder's face on screen 1 or 2. (C-sites-16)
- Only two of six put a contact action on the first mobile screen (Reuta's floating call pill; Galit Sharon's top bar and form under the hero); the rest put it one popup away or at the end of a 7–13k px page. (C-sites-17)
- A multi-offer solo practice splits offers in a hamburger menu by offer type or by audience, and on the home page gives each offer its own block with its own CTA (Reuta: mentoring tracks / courses / about / content / contact; Shelly Sever: verb-labelled groups). (C-sites-18)
- Podcast and content sit in a separate "content" group, never the lead; email capture is a free download or a footer field, not a newsletter pitch. (C-sites-19)
- Four of six set mobile body text at 18–20 px with 1.5–1.9 leading — a sample contrast with the studios' ~14 px, not a rule. (C-sites-20, C-sites-10)
- In one or two unthrottled headless runs, DoGood was lightest (about 1.1 MB, the only one under 1.5 MB), Shelly Sever and Reuta about 2 MB, and Rinat Hadar, the only site with autoplay video, heaviest and unstable (10–20 MB); five of six run on WordPress. (C-sites-21)
- Friction seen: Reuta's call pill shows 050-200-0224 but dials 055-985-1686; Shelly Sever's first screen is an intro loader plus a cookie banner with no offer text; Tali Gilad hides her hero sentence at 390 px; Reuta's lead popup covers most of the viewport later in the scroll; fixed reCAPTCHA and accessibility buttons sit over page edges (headless, one visit). (C-sites-22)

## Key players

**Israeli set**
- Nonprofit-only players (TikTek, Shepaz, Shiferon) are marketing agencies selling execution — content, campaigns, video, fundraising — with brand strategy at most one line. (C-players-01)
- "Branding and websites for nonprofits and businesses" is the most crowded claim: Studio Josef, Be Brand and Tali Gilad list nonprofits as one sector among many. (C-players-02)
- DoGood Studio ("מיתוג ודיגיטל לארגונים עם משמעות", branding and digital for organisations with meaning) is the only Israeli studio found that positions solely on purpose; Hebrew only, no named clients or awards on the home page. (C-players-03)
- The three Israeli personal-branding coaches opened (Shelly Sever, Galit Sharon, Reuta) frame it as career or self-presentation strategy; none sells visual identity or positions on impact. (C-players-05)
- Of the Hebrew branding courses opened, the designer-facing ones (Rinat Hadar, Tali Gilad) teach designers to brand for clients and the owner-facing ones (Mateg, Shelly Sever) teach personal branding with no impact framing; no Hebrew course for purpose-driven owners building their own brand was found. (C-players-06)
- Brand the Change lists exactly one certified trainer for Israel: Hadas Drachli, the client's founder (Brand Strategy, Personal Branding; sustainability, SMEs). No Israeli competitor opened cites an external purpose-branding method. (C-players-07)
- Among the 10 Israeli players in the set, proof is client logos and Google reviews and none shows an award; larger Israeli studios (NotFromHere, Butcher) do show awards; the Israel Brand Award (run by Brand Academy) had a nonprofits-and-society category in 2024, won by GIRAF. (C-players-08, C-sites-08)
- Among the studios in the players set, none shows a podcast or newsletter on the pages read; podcasts belong to coaches (Shelly Sever, Reuta), to Fifty & Fifty, and to the client. (C-players-09)
- Hebrew-only sites are the norm for small Israeli players; English versions appear with agencies courting donors or international clients (Be Brand, Shepaz). (C-players-11)

**International aspiration set**
- Fifty & Fifty publishes a $15–50k project band, 8–12-week timelines, 4 Webbys and an Anthem Award, a podcast and a newsletter; Briteweb is B Corp and Living Wage certified; Studio J Lorne quantifies outcomes (50+ brands, 3X average ROI, $50M+ client funding); Sereth serves nonprofits only; Brand the Change (Nairobi, 2014) teaches rather than serves agency clients. Of those checked, only Briteweb shows B Corp. (C-players-10)

**Crowded vs rare** (from the players lane's view, sourced per player above)
- Crowded: "branding + website for nonprofits and businesses", "we know nonprofits", proof by client logos or Google reviews, Hebrew-only sites. (C-players-01, C-players-02, C-players-08, C-players-11)
- Rare: personal branding and strategic brand planning from one purpose-positioned provider; a named, certified purpose-branding method; a studio with a podcast and courses; any visible studio price. (C-players-03, C-players-04, C-players-05, C-players-07, C-players-09)

## The client's position

- The site is Wix with 24 sitemap pages, 66 blog posts and 9 blog categories; only 8 pages are reachable from the menu, and 12 pages are linked from no other page. (C-position-01)
- Personal branding lives on `/personal101`, labelled "פיתוח עסק עם משמעות" (developing a business with meaning) in the menu and titled differently again in search; it describes four stages (SENSING, THINKING, MAKING, INFLUENCING), says stages one and two take about 10–15 meetings, offers a free Zoom intro call (Calendly) and three testimonials, and shows no price. (C-position-02)
- Strategic planning lives on `/process`, labelled "תהליך המיתוג" (the branding process); it describes **three** stages, claims "20 שנות אסטרטגיה, עיצוב ומיתוג" (20 years of strategy, design and branding), has one Calendly CTA, and no price, testimonial or case study, in long unbroken paragraphs. (C-position-03)
- Prices appear only on courses and workshops, all in shekels, and conflict: the same 8-meeting professional course is 2,850 ₪ on one page and 5,200 ש"ח + VAT on another; the currency is written four ways and VAT is stated inconsistently. (C-position-04)
- At least nine pages are stale, empty, broken or Wix template leftovers (ended courses, past events, a 2020–21 scholarship, San Francisco template events, a broken `/blank-2`; footer "@2019"). (C-position-05)
- Three pairs of pages duplicate each other (about, the personal-branding course, the professional course). (C-position-06)
- The home badge reads "1st PLACE — Branding for non profit organizations"; `/newcourse` says the studio won the Israeli branding award for 2022 in the non-profit category, and the team block credits the same award to designer Dana Russo. No page names the competition, and no winners list was found. (C-position-07)
- About 15 client or partner logos in one strip, 13 identifiable from alt text (including Haifa Municipality, Fairtrade Israel, Reichman University, Mandel Institute, Kfar Yona, עמותת ברטלי נוער בתנועה), with no case studies or project pages. (C-position-08, C-audience-12)
- Whether any of the public and academic logos (Haifa Municipality, Haifa cities association, Hof HaCarmel, Kfar Yona, Reichman, Mandel Institute) was paid strategy or branding work is unknown. (C-audience-12)
- About 9 testimonials across 4 pages, several in English, none dated; no off-site reviews found; Apple Podcasts shows 0 ratings. (C-position-09)
- The Brand the Change certified-trainer credential checks out off-site; the site gives two different reach figures for the method (5 languages / 40+ countries vs 6 languages / 36 countries). (C-position-10, C-players-07)
- The podcast is the only regular content asset: 23 episodes, April 2022 – September 2026 (8 / 5 / 6 / 1 / 3 per year); the site's podcast page stops at June 2024 and is linked from nowhere. (C-position-11)
- The blog has 66 posts; by sitemap lastmod (update dates) about 22 in 2020 against 3–5 a year since 2024; the three newest are from 2026; topics are mostly editorial. A monthly newsletter sign-up ("מגזין ׳מיטיב׳") sits in every footer. (C-position-12)
- Off-site presence is small: Instagram (169 followers, 27 posts), a personal LinkedIn, no Facebook page or Google Business Profile linked. (C-position-13)
- The page head is weak: the home `<title>` starts in English, there is no home meta description, titles use "brandforthebetter", `<h1>` is used for footer labels, and the brand name is plain Hebrew ("a beneficial brand"), a poor search key. Rankings are unmeasured. (C-position-14)
- There is no single primary action: a `/contact` form, Calendly links, WhatsApp mentioned without a link, a phone and an email on another domain (Hadas@kapara.com), plus payment links. (C-position-15)
- A free PDF lead magnet ("מדריך הכנה למיתוג", a branding-preparation guide) exists, but the page that hands it over is orphaned and no sign-up form for it was found. (C-position-16)

## The audience and what it searches for

Search terms are Google autocomplete completions (hl=iw, gl=il, 2026-10): they show which phrasings Google recognises, not volume.

**Segments**
- Purpose-driven solo professionals who want meaning in their work — the client's own stated buyer ("א.נשים המחפשים לשלב משמעות ועשייה מיטיבה בעבודה", people looking to combine meaning and beneficial action in their work); this is the client's statement, not independent evidence of who buys. (C-audience-04)
- One career adviser's 500-person survey (method unknown), reported by Maariv in July 2026, claims a 60 % rise in Israelis changing jobs since October 2023, with meaning among the stated wants; it is not evidence of a trigger for buying personal branding. (C-audience-05)
- Most Israeli self-employed women run solo businesses (86 % employ no one) on low incomes (2023 median ₪5,251 a month), and in Adva's sample 60–70 % lost income in each crisis; spend on branding is unmeasured. (C-audience-06)
- Social businesses: the NII's evaluation of employment-focused social businesses (2022) records marketing and packaging support given to them, and benefit-company status and the social model used as marketing assets; their completions are category terms ("עסק חברתי", "מיזם חברתי", "יזמות חברתית", "חברה לתועלת הציבור"). (C-audience-08)
- Amutot: completions are sector-named ("מיתוג עמותה", "מיתוג לעמותות", "לוגו לעמותה", "בניית אתר לעמותה"); strategy-for-amuta phrasings return nothing; GuideStar Israel profiles are used by funders and government support committees in grant decisions. (C-audience-11)
- Public bodies: "מיתוג עירוני" (municipal branding) and "מכרז מיתוג" (branding tender) are completions; the client's logo strip includes public and academic bodies, paid work unknown. (C-audience-12)
- Organisations booking talks search by format and audience ("הרצאות לארגונים", "הרצאה לעובדים", "סדנה לצוות"), not by topic: "הרצאה על מיתוג", "על אימפקט", "על קיימות" return nothing. (C-audience-13)
- SMBs "adding ESG" are not visible as a Hebrew search audience; ESG completions are corporate and conference names. (C-audience-14)

**What the leading services are called in search**
- Bare "מיתוג אישי" completes almost entirely to personalised merchandise or events (9 of 9 for the bare prefix); only "בלינקדאין" (on LinkedIn) among the "ב…" completions is professional. (C-audience-01)
- The professional sense has qualified phrasings: "מיתוג אישי לעסק", "בניית מותג אישי", "סדנת מיתוג אישי", "קורס מיתוג אישי", "יועץ מיתוג אישי", "מיתוג אישי מקצועי", "מה זה מיתוג אישי"; audience-specific forms (for therapists, freelancers, women) return nothing. (C-audience-02)
- "תכנון אסטרטגי", "תוכנית אסטרטגית" and "ייעוץ אסטרטגי" complete mostly to public-sector bodies, jobs and big consultancies; the business-owner phrasings that exist are "בניית אסטרטגיה עסקית / שיווקית / דיגיטלית", "אסטרטגיה שיווקית לעסק" and "חברת מיתוג ואסטרטגיה". (C-audience-03)

**Channels**
- LinkedIn reaches about 2.95 M Israelis, and "מיתוג אישי בלינקדאין" is a completion; nothing shows the client's buyers arrive through LinkedIn. (C-audience-07)

## What we do not know

- **Conversion.** No sales, enrolment or conversion data for any player, including the client. Every "what converts" line above is structure, not outcome. (skeptic, Pain vs WTP; C-sites-04, C-sites-05)
- **Which segment pays the client most**, and where its enquiries come from (LinkedIn, referrals, Facebook groups, accelerators). Not public.
- **Willingness to pay** for the two leading services. No price for strategic planning was found anywhere in Israel (industry lane, Dead ends); personal-branding prices rest on one consultant and a few courses (C-industry-02, C-players-04).
- **The award.** Its name and organiser, whether the studio or Dana Russo won it, and the project. Brand Academy runs the Israel Brand Award and had a nonprofits category in 2024 (C-players-08), a plausible organiser, unconfirmed (C-position-07).
- **Whether the public-sector and academic logos were paid work** or lectures and workshops (C-audience-12, C-position-08).
- **Which current-site offers are live**, and whether the 3-stage and 4-stage method descriptions are one method or two products (C-position-03, C-position-05).
- **Current prices.** The site's prices conflict and may be stale (C-position-04, C-position-05).
- **Google Business Profile and Facebook.** Neither could be checked from here (C-position-13).
- **Rankings.** No Google Israel search or Search Console data (C-position-14).
- **Buyer fears** (greenwashing, cost, "fluff"). No source speaks for buyers; the one claim that tried was killed (Appendix). The surviving context is consumer doubt about green messaging (C-industry-10).
- **Current Israeli B Corp count and current ESG rules (2024–26)** (C-industry-12, C-industry-13).
- **Whether Israeli buyers look abroad** at international purpose studios or at Brand the Change's own English courses (skeptic, Ignored competitors).
- **Gender of the client's buyers.** Nothing proves they are mostly women (audience lane, handoff).
- **IS 5568's exact WCAG version** and the turnover exemption thresholds (C-sites-14); the playbook's WCAG 2.1 AA floor covers either.
