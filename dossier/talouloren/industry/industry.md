## The industry and how it makes money

Not researched in this run (plan.md, "Not investigating"). What the reference set shows about the money path only:

- A free diagnostic with a stated time cost and payoff is the common first step; RND, Dent/KPI and Phable each name the minutes, "free" and a score or report next to the start button. (C-references-13)
- The step after the diagnostic is a call that goes through the result with the client. In this set it is free at RND and Dent, and paid only at Fascinate ($79 test, then a $697 package with two 45-minute sessions). No site credits a session fee against a later package. (C-references-14, C-references-15)
- Brand Builders Group routes its "free strategy call" through a scored ~10-screen application rather than a calendar. (C-references-16)
- TalouLoren's own past prices, as context only: a 60-minute paid session at 1,000 NIS (older copies 1,500, 690, 199 NIS), the BRAND YOURSELF course at 990 NIS incl. VAT against a "full price" of 1,755, and a 2019 programme of a 700 NIS intro plus four meetings at 8,000 NIS. (C-position-05, C-position-06, C-position-07)

## Sites in this space and what converts

No conversion data stands behind any line here. These are the structures of the sites studied.

**The diagnostic**
- RND's Hebrew business check is the closest working model of the Focused Check: 8 questions, one per screen, 4 option cards, a disabled Continue until an answer is picked, a "1 / 8" counter and gold progress bar on #131313, the contact gate after the last question ("your results are ready"), a score out of 100 over 8 categories, and one next step, a free fit meeting with the named consultant. 18 taps to the first submit; 1.09 MB. (C-references-11)
- The opposite gate also exists: Dent's ScoreApp scorecard takes name, email and phone before question 1, then 40 yes/no questions and a PDF. No data says which gate converts better. (C-references-12, C-references-26)
- Benny Vazana's Hebrew diagnostic is framed as an 8-stage game with a video per stage and a budget question at the end; 10.5 MB. (C-references-18)
- The coach-site register the brief rules out is common in the set: stale countdown timers on 2022 workshops, "last chance", "88% of our clients…", a "critical state — needs immediate intervention" score band, "8-figure", double exclamation marks. (C-references-27)

**People and proof**
- Of nine sites, the five that sell a person or a partnership show faces with names or titles on the first screen (Ashcroft's "LED BY" strip of three partners, Ninia, DG's two founders, Briony, BBG); the four that sell a studio's output do not. (C-references-24, C-references-06, C-references-19)
- Ninia Azzopardi puts the person first and the blue-chip logo wall second. (C-references-05)
- Two-partner studios Porto Rocha and Order carry Tinder, MoMA PS1, Google, Apple and Coca-Cola as named project rows with one line each, and put the founders in the news feed. (C-references-23)
- Video testimonials: BBG uses quote cards with a "watch video" link; DG uses 16:9 TV-clip thumbnails; Lush uses text-quote carousels. No common pattern. (C-references-17)
- No site in the set runs a vertical (9:16) video library. (C-references-28)

**Hebrew and RTL**
- Forward arrows point left on CTAs ("בוא נתחיל ←", let's start); well-built carousels compute `direction: rtl`, though DG and RND mix in `ltr` widgets. (C-references-20)
- None of four Hebrew sites isolates Latin brand words with `dir=ltr` / `lang=en`, and none publishes `hreflang`. Only DG has English, on a separate subdomain. (C-references-21)
- Body text runs 16–18 px with 1.33–2.0 line height in one Hebrew-capable family (Noto Sans Hebrew, Assistant, Rubik, atlas); mobile h1 22–29 px. (C-references-22)
- Lush shows a calm editorial RTL pattern: logotype, one belief sentence, numbered services, portfolio tiles; but 60 MB. (C-references-25)
