What a guest meets today, and what the preview does instead

1. Four rooms that read the same

On your Rooms page, the Double Deluxe, the Suite Triple, the Family Villa and the Luxury Private Villa all open with the same sentence: “Situated in Ahangama Central, 300 metres from Kabalana Beach…”. That's the general description from your Booking.com listing. What makes each room different (22 to 50 m², king or twin beds, a sofa bed, an outdoor bath, a private pool) is only on your booking page.

In the preview, each room has its own card. Tap one and you see its size, how many it sleeps, the beds, what's special about it and its own photos. Then there's one button to that room on your booking page.

2. The scores you've earned aren't on your site

On Google and Booking.com, guests rate Panta Rei 4.7 from 213 reviews and 9.2 from 468. Your website doesn't show either score. Your booking page shows 4.8 on Google and 9.1 on Booking.com, which no longer match today's scores.

In the preview, the two scores have their own section, each with the number of reviews and the date we read it. Below them are six guests' reviews, quoted word for word with their name and where they wrote it. The Booking.com score also appears again next to the dates, at the moment a guest decides.

3. No idea of the price until the booking page

A guest comparing places on a phone sees room names but no prices. To find one, they have to go to the booking page and choose dates. Until then it says “Add dates for prices”.

Your rates change with the dates, so the preview doesn't pretend to have one fixed price. What it does is let the guest choose the room, arrival, departure and number of guests on your own page. “See prices on our booking page” then opens that room on your booking page with those dates already filled in, so the price for their stay is the first thing they see. With JavaScript switched off, every room card is still a plain link to its page on your booking page.

4. The email address goes to other inboxes

Your pages show hello@pantareiresort.com. On Contact and in the footer, tapping it opens a message to pantareiresort@gmail.com. In the Activities footer it opens a message to info@mysite.com, an example address that came with the website template, so a guest writing from there won't reach you.

The preview shows one address, hello@pantareiresort.com, and the link writes to that address. If you'd rather guests wrote to the Gmail address, tell us and we'll switch it.

5. A heavy first load on a local SIM

Before a guest scrolls, your home page on a phone downloads about 15.7 MB, mostly the background film and full-size photos. It also contacts nine other companies' servers, including error-tracking and visitor-analytics services. That's a lot of data for someone on a Sri Lankan SIM.

The preview loads 653 KB first. Your film then starts once the page is already showing, at 1.5 MB on a phone. Photos are sized for each screen, and nothing loads from anyone else's server: no trackers and no pop-ups.

6. And the things that were in PDFs

The Surf Therapy Massage (“full recovery after active surfing”), the fitness class prices and the pool day pass were only inside PDF files. To read them, a guest has to download and open a file. In the preview they're written on the page: the massage with its 60 and 45 minute prices, the Vinyasa class every morning at 8:30 except Friday with the three class prices, and the pool day pass. The restaurant has its own section too, with the breakfast and later-on dishes and the opening hours.

Also worth doing, outside the website

  • Facebook: your page lists +94 74 209 5135 and pantareiresort@gmail.com, while your site and Google say +94 72 800 3022 and hello@pantareiresort.com. One set everywhere saves a guest guessing which one to use.
  • Your booking page: the logo at the top links to an address with no ending (“pantareiresort.holidayfuture.”), so a guest there can't tap back to your website. The rating line there could also be updated to 4.7 and 9.2.
  • Full rooms on your booking page: when a guest arrives with dates the room can't take, the booking page clears the dates without saying why. It may be worth asking your booking-page provider whether it can show a “not available” message instead.
