A one-page preview for Sanjee Surf School, and what it would give up

sanjee-surf.com is a real, working site: 67 pages, tours added as recently as October 2026, and a dialog on the home page that sells a lesson with live dates, named times and a deposit. We are not writing because it is a bad site. We walked it on a phone on 5 November 2026, as a traveller would, and met a few things that can stop a visitor. So we built a one-page alternative from the school's published facts and photographs. You can open it now:

sanjee-surf-school.os1.workers.dev

Nobody asked us to make this. It is a private preview: search engines are told not to list it, and sanjee-surf.com and the Google listing stay exactly as they are. Nothing goes live unless you decide. If you would rather we took it down, tell us and we will.

We don't know who looks after the site or who decides for the school, so this page is written to the school, not to a person. If that is someone else, please pass it on.

The trade, first

The current site takes a booking inside the page. The preview ends in a question on WhatsApp. That is the biggest difference between them, and it is not an obvious win. It is yours to weigh.

From a phone to a booking, or to a question

Today: five taps, three typed fields and a card

"Book Your Lesson" opens a four-step dialog: lessons or tuk-tuk, then the offer, then a date, then a time. Then the visitor types a name, an email and a phone number, ticks the terms and presses "Pay $8 Now". The dialog has no WhatsApp or phone link. Its only contact is an email address on the date step, for someone who sees no time that works.

The preview: one tap to a chat

"Ask on WhatsApp" is on the first screen and in a bar that stays at the bottom of the phone screen. One tap opens a chat with a message already started. If the visitor has chosen a lesson, a date or how many people, the message carries them. The links work with JavaScript switched off. We read every link's number and words, but we did not open WhatsApp or send anything.

These are not two scores for the same thing. The five taps end with a held time. The one tap ends with a question that someone still has to answer.

What a visitor meets today, and what the preview does instead

Everything here is what we saw on 5 November 2026, walking the site in a browser set up as an iPhone, not logged in to anything. Sometimes the preview simply says nothing until the school decides, and we say so.

A red box over the first screen

Today

About six seconds after the home page opens, a red box saying "Could not refresh surf conditions" covers the top of the first screen for about five seconds and runs off its right edge. Further down, the surf report says "Live data temporarily unavailable".

How far this goes: it showed on all 3 fresh loads we made, at 09:05, 09:10 and 09:24 UTC, from one machine on one network, in an automated browser. We have not tried it from elsewhere or on a real phone. The reason we think other visitors get it too is in the code, not in a test: the page asks its surf-conditions service again about every 130 ms after each good answer, until the server refuses it (41 and 32 good answers on the two loads we logged), and the refusal brings up the box. On a slower connection it comes later.

For whoever maintains it: in the script bundle the fetch callback depends on the data state, and the effect runs whenever the callback changes, so each success fetches again. It is worth fixing whatever you decide about the rest of this page, because it also keeps calling the service.

On the preview

No surf report and no live data, so this can't happen. It also means there is no surf report.

What the preview is, and its limits

  • It is a finished one-page build, and nothing more yet. It runs on a temporary address, with no editor, no login and no domain.
  • It has not been tested on a real iPhone or a range of Android phones. Our measurements ran in a browser imitating a phone, with software graphics. Beyond that, one of us has looked at the design on one phone, at the mockup stage.
  • In that imitated slow phone the first load was 373 KB with 0 requests to outside servers, and the main photograph showed in about 2 seconds. That is a measure of the preview alone. We did not run the current site under the same conditions.
  • It leaves things out on purpose: opening hours, cancellation terms, a reply time, the rental deposit amount and video. The list near the end says why.
  • Every fact on it traces to sanjee-surf.com or the Google listing, and our reviewer found 0 mismatches against them. That is not the same as correct. Where those sources disagree we had to choose, and only the school can confirm the choice.
  • The photographs are the school's own, from its site and its Google listing, and there are 8 of them. None is stock.

Also worth doing, whatever you decide

  • Set the hours on Google. Google shows 9 am to 5 pm every day. The home page says 6AM to 6PM, the rental page 06:00 to 18:30, and the dialog offers a 6:00 AM Sunrise Session. Someone who found the school on Maps would not expect a dawn lesson.
  • Reopen the Instagram, Facebook and YouTube accounts, or take the links off. That includes the "or message us on Instagram" line on the rental page, for as long as that page is up.

How it could run: you choose

Nothing is set up yet: no hosting, no login, no domain. We set it up once you choose. Managed is the one we recommend: we host the site and keep it running, and hosting, updates and backups are our job. You pay a monthly fee that we agree with you, and you edit your content whenever you like. Self-hosted: we install the same site and editor on a server of your own for a one-time fee that we agree with you. It is then fully yours. You pay your server company roughly $6–12 a month, and you take care of updates and backups yourself. Files only: if you never want to use an editor, we hand you the finished site as files that run on any web host; every change then needs a developer.

The preview runs on a temporary address. If the new site is to go on sanjee-surf.com, we connect it once you have chosen, and it would replace what is there now, including the dialog, unless that moves to an address of its own.

You can also choose none of these. Everything on this page about the current site is yours to use either way.

What only the school can settle before it goes live

The preview repeats what sanjee-surf.com and Google say. None of these is a fault. They are simply things we can't know from outside.

  1. WhatsApp. Is +94 77 400 4422 the right number for lesson questions, and who answers it? The FAQ says replies come "within a few hours". The preview doesn't repeat that until you confirm it.
  2. Times and deposits. Should the message offer a time of day? Should a deposit be asked for over WhatsApp, and how much? And which rental deposit is right? The pages disagree, so the preview says "Ask us how much it is".
  3. Opening hours. Three sets are published, so the preview shows none. Which is right?
  4. Cancellation. Which policy applies, if any, once there is no deposit on the page?
  5. Prices and group lessons. The preview uses the home page's prices as of 5 November 2026. Are they current, and is there a group lesson to show?
  6. How many people on a tour. The pages give up to 2 and up to 3. The preview says "A tour's price is for the tour, not per person. Ask us how many people can come." What should it say?
  7. Two photographs. The action shot of Sanjee on the yellow board: who took it? Other photos on the Google listing carry a photographer's watermark, so if this one is theirs too, we would need their agreement. The "Total beginner" picture shows an instructor and a student from a clip on the Google listing: are the people in it happy to be shown? The site's Terms promise explicit consent for that.
  8. "30 seconds from the sand". The preview repeats this line from the rental page. The Google pin sits 105 m from the shore, though it may not mark the shop. Should the line stay? And is it Coco Beach or Coconut Beach? The site uses both.
  9. Instructor certificates. The preview prints ISA Level 1 Surf Instructor and Certified Surf Lifeguard, as the About page does. We found no certificate or registry entry to check them against, so we would like to see them, or have you confirm them, before go-live.
  10. Sanjee's words. The preview shortens the first-person story from the About page and calls him head instructor. Is Sanjee happy with both?
  11. AI assistants. Once live, assistants such as ChatGPT could read the site and quote it to travellers. Keep that, or block the ones that train on it and keep the ones that answer live questions?
  12. More material, if you have it. There is no video on the preview: the two clips on the Google listing could not be downloaded. We also found no large photograph of a lesson, and no names for the other instructors. Send what you want shown.
