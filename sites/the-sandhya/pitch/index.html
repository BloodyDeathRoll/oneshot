What a guest meets today, and what the preview does instead

We walked thesandhya.com on a phone on 6 November 2026, as a guest would, without booking anything. Our screenshots of thesandhya.com below are from that walk.

A white page on a slow connection

Today

On a slowed-down 4G connection the home page stays white, and tapping RESERVE does nothing for about 52 seconds. Even on a fast line a white layer sits over RESERVE for 7.5 seconds after the page is ready: a tap in that time does nothing.

On the preview

Nothing covers the page. The words and the booking button are usable before the photograph arrives, and booking still works with JavaScript switched off.

The dates in the panel are not sent

Today

RESERVE opens a panel that already shows today, tomorrow and 2 adults. If the guest keeps those dates and taps RESERVE, the booking page receives empty dates, and the number of adults is never sent at all.

On the preview

No dates are filled in for the guest. Under the rooms, and again in the booking section, the guest can pick a check-in and a check-out date. Once both are picked, every booking button names them ("Check 8–11 Jan · 3 nights") and adds them to the link to your booking page. With no dates picked, the buttons go to your booking page as they do today.

The room the guest chose is lost

Today

A guest who reads about the Director's Suite and taps RESERVE NOW follows the same link as every other room, with no room in it.

On the preview

Each of the four room types has its photographs, its bed, size and floor, and its own button. That button's link carries the room's own number from your booking engine, so it can open on that room (see "Before it goes live" below).

The first screen does not say what the hotel is

Today

The first screen gives a dictionary entry for the word Sandhya and "Adults Only Hideaway". A guest scrolls about two screens to read "hotel"; Sri Lanka first appears about 3,200 pixels down the home page, Kabalana about 3,900, and Ahangama only in the menu and the footer.

On the preview

The first screen is your photograph of a room open to the sea, with one line, "An adults-only beachfront boutique hotel on Kabalana Beach, Ahangama, Sri Lanka", and the booking button. The Sanskrit meaning of your name, twilight, has its place near the end, under "Why Sandhya".

No rating or guest review

Today

None of the 10 pages we read shows a rating, a review or a link to one. A guest who wants proof leaves for a listing.

On the preview

Your 9.7 out of 10 on Trip.com, with the review count, the date we read it and a link, straight after the rooms, and two guest reviews quoted whole, with their source and month.

Calling, writing and finding the hotel

Today

The phone number is a call link and the email a mail link, both 15 to 16 pixels tall. The location page shows a drawn map of the island, and no page links to a map, so a guest copies the address into a maps app themselves.

On the preview

Call, Email, Map and Instagram are buttons 50 pixels tall, with the address, the phone number and the email printed beside them. Map opens your Google listing. A round call button sits in the bar at the bottom of the phone screen.

Also worth doing, away from the website

  • Your Google listing. "Check availability" and "Compare prices" come first and list resellers (Agoda and Hotels.com on one load, Hotels.com and Expedia on another); your own booking page was not among them, and the link to thesandhya.com sits below. Ask Cloudbeds whether your direct rate can appear in that price list.
  • Your MICHELIN Guide page. It shows a fixed bar reading "We're not currently taking reservations for this hotel. Please explore other hotels in our selection.", and it has no link to your own site. Ask the Guide why, and ask them to link thesandhya.com.

Trip.com says check-in after 16:00, no pets, and children aged 12 and above welcome, while you describe the hotel as adults only. Once you have settled check-in, the age rule and pets, make Google, Trip.com and your booking engine say the same thing.

What you will be able to change yourself

Today the preview is a finished build, with no login. With the managed or the self-hosted option below, you get your own login to a simple admin page that shows only The Sandhya. You fill in fields, not code, so an edit cannot break the design. Press Publish and the site rebuilds itself.

  • Text the opening line, the closing line, and every section: the rooms, the Dining Room, Casual Luxe, Kabalana and around, Exclusive Use, Why Sandhya
  • Rooms each room's name, bed, guests, description and main photograph
  • Photos the first screen and the photographs through the page, with their descriptions. Upload from your phone; the site makes the sizes itself, and you mark the point of each picture that its crops centre on.
  • Stay check-in and check-out times, the amenities, the front-desk hours
  • Prices the Exclusive Use price
  • Reviews remove any rating or guest review; we add new ones with their source
  • Contact and booking phone, email, address, map link, Instagram, and the links into your booking engine

The layout, colours and fonts stay fixed. A few parts of this design have no field yet: the room galleries beyond each room's first photograph, the "Every room has" list, the logos and the date fields. We change those for you when you ask.

How it can run: you choose

Nothing is set up yet: no hosting, no login, no domain. We set it up once you choose. Managed is the one we recommend: we host the site and keep it running, and hosting, updates and backups are our job. You pay a monthly fee that we agree with you, and you edit your content whenever you like. Self-hosted: we install the same site and editor on a server of your own for a one-time fee that we agree with you. It is then fully yours. You pay your server company roughly $6–12 a month, and you take care of updates and backups yourself. Files only: if you never want to edit it yourself, we hand you the finished site as files that run on any web host; every change then needs a developer.

Your domain, thesandhya.com, stays yours. Once you have chosen, we connect it to the new site.

Before it goes live, we need you to confirm

  1. That your booking page reads the link. Your Cloudbeds page does not open for our automated test browser, so we could not see what it does with the dates and the room that the preview adds to the link. Together we open each room's button, with and without dates, in a normal browser. If the booking page ignores them, we take the date fields out rather than show dates that are not used.
  2. Check-in and the age rule. The preview gives check-in from 14:00 and check-out by 12:00, as your booking engine does, and says that other sites differ. Tell us the times and the minimum age, and we print them plainly.
  3. The ratings and reviews shown. We read every score from public listings on 6 November 2026. Tell us which you want on the site, and we keep the date and source beside each one.
  4. The Director's Suite bed. The preview says King bed. Your own room page says Super King.
  5. A few photographs. One Studio Twin photograph whose room we could not tell for certain, and the three photographs of your staff, which we show only with your agreement. The first screen on a phone is cut from a smaller copy of your photograph; the original file would make it sharper.
  6. WhatsApp. Your site shows a WhatsApp icon beside the phone number, but it is not a link, and we could not confirm that the number takes messages. The preview leaves WhatsApp out. Tell us if guests should use it.
  7. AI assistants. Once live, assistants such as ChatGPT can read the site and quote it to travellers. Keep that, or block the ones that train on it?
